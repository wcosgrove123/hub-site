Warm-up 4 min

Context sentence for the debate, written alone: what happened between 1816 and 1828 that made North, South, and West want different things from Washington? Use two of: Tariff of 1816, Second Bank, Panic of 1819, Missouri Compromise, American System. Two volunteers read; the class names what makes one earn the point.

Objectives, agenda, homework 2 min

By the end of today you can

  • Compare what the North, South, and West wanted from the federal government on the tariff, the Bank, and Indian policy (CED 4.3).
  • Explain how a region's economy shaped its position on one issue (point of view, skill 2.B).
  • Situate the sectional debates of 1828 in a broader context, 1816–1828, using two specific developments.

Agenda

Objectives. How the debate is scored. Tonight's reading is the Jackson chapter.

Homework

Read Ch. 14, 276–304 (new book Ch. 13, 284–310): nullification, the Bank, removal.

Contextualization

Folded into the warm-up today: students write the context and the teacher names what earns the rubric point (more than a phrase or a reference).

Content 3 min

One slide, the trigger. Tariff of 1828, the Tariff of Abominations: who is protected and who pays. Calhoun's unsigned South Carolina Exposition and Protest (1828): a state may judge a federal law void. Recall the Virginia and Kentucky Resolutions.

Activity 30 min

Sectionalism debate (dept format). Opening statements, 2 minutes each: South, North, West, National (8 min). Then the floor opens by topic, about 4 minutes each: tariffs, national bank, Indian policy, states' rights vs. federal power, slavery. Transportation and industry vs. agriculture move to tomorrow's gallery walk if the clock runs out. Speakers may tag in a partner. Everyone not speaking fills the comparison grid (four regions down, topics across: what they want, and why their economy makes them want it). Scored with the six-behavior seminar code, 0 to 2.

Closure 5 min

Last row of the grid: which two regions could make a deal, and on what? Which issue has no deal? One sentence back to the QOTD.
