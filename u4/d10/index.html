If we run short, blocks cut to 32 minutes in all; the question, objectives and closure never cut.

Warm-up 12 min

Thesis drill (dept sheet). Prompt: Evaluate to what extent the role of transportation, agriculture, and innovation changed the U.S. economy between 1800–1840. Pods draft, 5 min. Rotate the paper: the next pod scores it on the 3-point thesis rubric (counterclaim 1, claim with an extent 1, two specific reasons 1) and fixes one thing, 4 min. Papers return: final thesis, 3 min. The department's frame (Although X; because A and B, therefore Y) and the class formula are the same sentence in a different order; show both once.

Objectives, agenda, homework 2 min

By the end of today you can

  • Construct a thesis that takes a position on the extent of change and names a counterargument (skill 6.A).
  • Critique another pod's thesis against the 3-point rubric and revise it.
  • Support an argument with specific and relevant evidence by outlining two body paragraphs with three pieces of evidence each (skill 6.B).

Contextualization

Inside the outline: context is its own box on the dept sheet.

Content 8 min

LEQ review (dept deck): the six rubric points in student words; the department's standard of six pieces of evidence; context means three or four sentences from before the prompt's dates; the sentence frame for reasoning (Evidence A is different from Evidence B because ..., which supports the argument that ...). The 2023 sample prompt on parties is there for students to read on their own.

Activity 18 min

Group LEQ outline (dept sheet) on the same prompt, from the pod's final thesis: unpack; brainstorm Changes and Continuities (from the stars and the circle on the gallery-walk chart); context bullets; two body paragraphs with three pieces of evidence each; where the counterargument goes. One outline per pod, turned in.

Closure 4 min

Each pod names the weakest box on its own outline. Alone: one thing you will do differently on Wednesday's pre-write. QOTD: what did your pod count as proof of change?
