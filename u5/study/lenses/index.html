Lenses

12 big terms, each seen through all eight themes.

The eight themes are the College Board's eight ways of looking at U.S. history. Each line says what the term means for one theme. A line marked Stretch fits its theme only loosely. Is it really a stretch? Argue it either way.

  • Manifest Destiny

    NAT

    Cast Americans as a chosen people with a mission to spread democracy, and drew lines around who was fit to share it.

    WXT

    Promised farmland, gold and Pacific ports for trade with Asia.

    GEO

    Treated the whole continent, to the Pacific, as the nation's natural boundary.

    MIG

    Encouraged hundreds of thousands to move west on the Oregon and California trails.

    PCE

    Polk and the Democrats used it to justify annexing Texas and fighting Mexico.

    WOR

    Pushed the U.S. into war with Mexico and hard bargaining with Britain over Oregon.

    ARC

    Echoed in Hudson River School landscapes and paintings of westward progress.

    SOC

    Assumed Anglo-Americans were superior to the Native people and Mexicans already living in the West.

  • Mexican–American War

    NAT

    Raised whether conquest fit a republic, and whether Mexicans in the new lands could become Americans.

    WXT

    Won California's harbors, and its gold, found nine days before the treaty was signed.

    GEO

    Moved the border to the Rio Grande and added California and the Southwest.

    MIG

    Opened the Southwest and California to American settlement.

    PCE

    The Wilmot Proviso split both major parties by section.

    WOR

    The first U.S. war fought mainly on foreign soil, against a neighboring republic.

    ARC

    Thoreau's essay and Douglass's editorials turned opposition into lasting writing.

    SOC

    Mexicans and Native people in the ceded lands lost land, rights and self-sufficiency.

  • California Gold Rush

    NAT

    Drew people from around the world and tested who could claim California's wealth.

    WXT

    Created a boom economy of mining, shipping, banking and supply in San Francisco.

    GEO

    Mining tore up riverbeds and hillsides and fouled the water.

    MIG

    Hundreds of thousands arrived from the eastern U.S., Latin America, Europe and China.

    PCE

    California's fast application to enter as a free state set off the crisis of 1850.

    WOR

    Tied the U.S. to Pacific trade and drew immigrants from China.

    ARC

    Stretch Built a rough, mixed mining culture told in letters, stories and songs.

    SOC

    Foreign Miners' Taxes and violence pushed out Mexican, Chinese and Native people.

  • Second Great Awakening

    NAT

    Tied American identity to evangelical Protestantism and a mission to improve the nation.

    WXT

    Spread fast in market towns, where preachers urged the sobriety and discipline that new wage work demanded.

    GEO

    Burned through western New York along the Erie Canal and through frontier camp meetings.

    MIG

    Followed settlers west; persecuted Mormons moved all the way to Utah.

    PCE

    Converts organized petitions and voluntary societies that pressed governments for reform.

    WOR

    Stretch Missionary and Bible societies sent preachers and Bibles abroad.

    ARC

    Camp meetings, emotional preaching and new churches (Methodist, Baptist, AME, Mormon) remade American religion.

    SOC

    Women and free Black Americans found new roles as leaders in churches and reform.

  • Seneca Falls Convention

    NAT

    Rewrote the Declaration of Independence to include women: “all men and women are created equal.”

    WXT

    Demanded married women's rights to their own property and wages, and women's access to trades and professions.

    GEO

    Stretch Met in the reform-heavy canal country of upstate New York.

    MIG

    Stretch Little direct link to migration.

    PCE

    Its most debated demand was the vote, which no state allowed women.

    WOR

    Stretch Grew from the London World Anti-Slavery Convention, where women delegates were shut out (1840).

    ARC

    Grew out of reform culture and the Quaker practice of women speaking in meeting.

    SOC

    Challenged separate spheres and the legal subordination of married women.

  • Irish immigration

    NAT

    Nativists asked whether Catholics could be loyal Americans.

    WXT

    Supplied cheap labor for canals, railroads, docks and domestic service.

    GEO

    Clustered in Northeastern port cities such as Boston and New York.

    MIG

    More than a million fled the famine of 1845 to 1852.

    PCE

    Irish voters joined Democratic city machines; nativists answered with the Know-Nothings.

    WOR

    Pushed out by famine and British policy in Ireland.

    ARC

    Brought Catholic churches and parish schools, and fights over the Bible in public schools.

    SOC

    Faced job discrimination and competed with free Black workers at the bottom of Northern wages.

  • King Cotton

    NAT

    Southern leaders made cotton and slavery the core of a separate Southern identity.

    WXT

    Cotton was the nation's leading export, grown by enslaved labor.

    GEO

    Spread across the Deep South's fertile soils as older Southeastern land wore out.

    MIG

    Drove the forced migration of about a million enslaved people to the Southwest.

    PCE

    Gave planters the confidence to demand protection for slavery and, in the end, to secede.

    WOR

    Southerners believed Britain's mills needed their cotton too much to let them fall.

    ARC

    Stretch A planter culture of honor and “the Southern way of life” grew around it.

    SOC

    Built a society with planters on top, yeomen in the middle and enslaved people at the bottom.

  • Compromise of 1850

    NAT

    Clay and Webster argued that saving the Union was worth painful bargains.

    WXT

    Stretch Paid off Texas's debts and closed the slave trade, not slavery, in the capital.

    GEO

    Made California free and organized Utah and New Mexico with no ban on slavery.

    MIG

    Answered the rush of migrants that filled California.

    PCE

    Passed as separate bills, by different majorities, steered through by Douglas.

    WOR

    Stretch Organized land just won from Mexico.

    ARC

    Its Fugitive Slave Act inspired Uncle Tom's Cabin.

    SOC

    The Fugitive Slave Act put free Black Northerners at risk of kidnapping.

  • Uncle Tom's Cabin

    NAT

    Asked readers whether a Christian nation could allow slavery.

    WXT

    A publishing sensation: about 300,000 copies sold in its first year.

    GEO

    Stretch Moves from Kentucky to the Louisiana cotton country, contrasting the Upper and Lower South.

    MIG

    Eliza's escape across the icy Ohio River dramatized flight north.

    PCE

    Hardened Northern opposition to the Fugitive Slave Act and pushed readers toward antislavery politics.

    WOR

    Translated widely; it shaped European views of American slavery.

    ARC

    The best-selling American novel of the century, answered by proslavery “anti-Tom” novels.

    SOC

    Showed slavery tearing families apart, speaking to women's moral authority as mothers.

  • Kansas-Nebraska Act

    NAT

    Asked whether the people of a territory or the nation as a whole should decide slavery.

    WXT

    Tied to plans for a railroad to the Pacific along a central route.

    GEO

    Opened land north of 36°30′ to slavery by erasing the Missouri line.

    MIG

    Set off a race of proslavery and antislavery settlers into Kansas.

    PCE

    Broke the Whig Party and gave rise to the Republicans.

    WOR

    Stretch Came the same year as the Ostend Manifesto, as Northerners grew wary of slavery's spread anywhere.

    ARC

    Stretch Set off a war of sermons, cartoons and songs, down to the rifles called “Beecher's Bibles.”

    SOC

    Made slavery's future depend on which side could out-settle, and outgun, the other.

  • Dred Scott v. Sandford

    NAT

    Declared that Black Americans could not be citizens.

    WXT

    Treated enslaved people as property the Constitution protected in every territory.

    GEO

    Opened every territory to slavery and struck down the Missouri line.

    MIG

    Scott's claim rested on having lived in free Illinois and Wisconsin Territory.

    PCE

    The Court tried to settle what Congress could not, and made it worse.

    WOR

    Stretch Little direct link to foreign affairs.

    ARC

    Stretch Became a rallying cry in Republican speeches and newspapers.

    SOC

    Made race a line no free Black person could cross into citizenship.

  • Election of 1860

    NAT

    Southern leaders treated the result of a legal election as a reason to leave the nation.

    WXT

    The Republican platform promised a protective tariff, homesteads and a Pacific railroad.

    GEO

    Results split by region: Lincoln took the free states, Breckinridge most slave states, Bell three Upper South states.

    MIG

    Stretch Free homesteads appealed to Western settlers and immigrants.

    PCE

    Lincoln won 180 electoral votes with about 40 percent of the popular vote and none from the South.

    WOR

    Stretch Southern leaders hoped Britain would back an independent cotton South.

    ARC

    Rallies, songs and marching “Wide Awake” clubs made it a mass campaign.

    SOC

    Fear for slavery's future drove the Deep South to secede.
