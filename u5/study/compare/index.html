Compare

19 comparisons, side by side.

Each card sets two or three things side by side, then says how they are alike and how they differ.

  • Who decides slavery in the territories?

    Wilmot Proviso (1846): Congress bans it.
    Popular sovereignty (1847–54): the settlers vote.
    Dred Scott (1857): Congress cannot ban it at all.
    Alike

    All three tried to settle the same question: slavery in land won from Mexico and beyond.

    Differ

    Each gave the decision to a different body (Congress, settlers, the Court), and each made the conflict worse.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Three compromises

    Missouri Compromise (1820)
    Compromise of 1850
    Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854)
    Alike

    Each tried to keep the Union by balancing slave and free interests.

    Differ

    1820 drew a line; 1850 dropped lines for popular sovereignty in Utah and New Mexico; 1854 erased the 1820 line.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Antislavery parties

    Liberty Party (1840): abolition through politics.
    Free Soil Party (1848): stop slavery's spread.
    Republican Party (1854): stop slavery's spread, plus tariffs, homesteads and railroads.
    Alike

    All took the fight against slavery into elections.

    Differ

    Each asked for less than the one before and won far more votes.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Abolitionist strategies

    Moral persuasion: Garrison, The Liberator.
    Politics and the Constitution: Douglass after 1851, the Liberty Party.
    Resistance and force: Garnet's “Address,” John Brown.
    Alike

    All wanted slavery ended, not just limited.

    Differ

    They split over whether to work through the government and whether violence was justified.

    SOC · Social Structures

  • Defenses of slavery

    Necessary evil (Jefferson, 1820)
    Positive good (Calhoun, 1837)
    Wage slavery and the mudsill (Fitzhugh, Hammond, 1850s)
    Alike

    All defended keeping slavery.

    Differ

    They moved from apology, to praise, to attacking Northern society itself.

    SOC · Social Structures

  • Two economies

    North: factories, wage labor, immigrants, railroads, banks.
    South: cotton and other staples, enslaved labor, few factories, few immigrants.
    Alike

    Both were tied together by cotton: Southern fields fed Northern and British mills.

    Differ

    Free labor and slave labor led to different ideas about land, work and the West.

    WXT · Work, Exchange, and Technology

  • Northern critics of the war with Mexico

    Lincoln's Spot Resolutions: the president misled Congress.
    Douglass in the North Star: a war for slaveholders.
    Thoreau: refuse to support an unjust government.
    Alike

    All opposed a war they saw as unjust.

    Differ

    Lincoln worked inside Congress, Douglass through the press, Thoreau through personal refusal.

    WOR · America in the World

  • Two awakenings

    First Great Awakening (1730s–40s)
    Second Great Awakening (1800–1840s)
    Alike

    Both were emotional Protestant revivals that challenged established churches.

    Differ

    The First fed revolutionary ideas; the Second stressed free will and fed reform movements.

    ARC · American and Regional Culture

  • Two declarations

    Declaration of Independence (1776)
    Declaration of Sentiments (1848)
    Alike

    Same form: a list of grievances against a ruling power and a claim of natural rights.

    Differ

    1848 named men, not a king, as the power, and added women to “all men are created equal.”

    NAT · American and National Identity

  • Two novels about slavery

    Uncle Tom's Cabin (Stowe, 1852)
    Aunt Phillis's Cabin (Eastman, 1852)
    Alike

    Both used fiction to win readers' feelings about slavery.

    Differ

    Stowe showed its cruelty; Eastman showed it as kind and orderly.

    ARC · American and Regional Culture

  • Irish and German immigrants

    Irish: famine refugees, mostly Catholic, settled in port cities, took low-wage jobs.
    German: varied faiths and classes, many farmed in the Midwest, some Forty-Eighters.
    Alike

    Both arrived in huge numbers in the 1840s and 1850s and built ethnic communities.

    Differ

    Different places, work and politics; the Irish faced the sharpest anti-Catholic hostility.

    MIG · Migration and Settlement

  • Two new parties of the 1850s

    Know-Nothings: against immigrants and Catholics.
    Republicans: against slavery's spread.
    Alike

    Both drew voters away from the collapsing Whigs and Democrats.

    Differ

    Nativism faded; free soil became the North's main cause, and Republicans absorbed many Know-Nothings.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Groups kept outside “the people,” 1820s–1850s

    Black Americans, enslaved and free
    American Indians
    Mexicans in the Cession, Chinese immigrants, women
    Alike

    Each lived under U.S. law but was denied full citizenship or rights, and each resisted.

    Differ

    Different tools: slave codes and Dred Scott; removal and reservations; land laws and People v. Hall; coverture and no vote.

    NAT · American and National Identity

  • Upper South and Lower South

    Upper South (Maryland, Virginia, Kentucky)
    Lower South (South Carolina to Texas)
    Alike

    Both were slave societies defended by their leaders.

    Differ

    Upper South had worn-out tobacco land, more free Black people, and sold people south; it seceded later or not at all.

    GEO · Geography and the Environment

  • Elections of 1856 and 1860

    1856: Buchanan wins; Frémont takes 11 Northern states.
    1860: Lincoln wins every free state but New Jersey's split vote, with no Southern electoral votes.
    Alike

    Both showed the Republicans as a party of the North only.

    Differ

    In 1860 the Democrats split, so Northern votes alone could win.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Utopias and refuges

    Brook Farm and Fruitlands (Transcendentalist)
    New Harmony and Oneida (social experiments)
    Shakers and Mormons (religious communities)
    Alike

    All left mainstream society to build a better one.

    Differ

    Different goals (thought, property, faith); the Mormons alone grew into a lasting society.

    SOC · Social Structures

  • What is the Union?

    Compact theory: South Carolina's 1860 declaration.
    Perpetual Union: Lincoln's First Inaugural (1861).
    Alike

    Both appealed to the founding and the Constitution.

    Differ

    One said states made the Union and may leave it; the other said the Union came first and cannot be broken.

    PCE · Politics and Power

  • Two Californios' and Tejanos' answers

    Mariano Vallejo: welcomed U.S. rule, then resented it.
    Juan Cortina: took up arms against Anglo abuses (1859).
    Alike

    Both describe the mistreatment of Mexicans after 1848.

    Differ

    Vallejo stayed inside the American system; Cortina broke with it.

    SOC · Social Structures

  • Three answers in 1850

    Clay and Webster: compromise to save the Union.
    Calhoun: guarantees for the South or disunion.
    Seward and Swisshelm: a “higher law” forbids bargaining over slavery.
    Alike

    All three responded to the crisis over California.

    Differ

    They disagreed about whether the Union, the South or conscience came first.
