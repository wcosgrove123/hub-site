College Board topics

The 12 topics this unit covers.

The College Board writes the AP exam from its course guide, the Course and Exam Description (CED). The guide splits the course into numbered topics: Topic 5.4 is the fourth topic of the guide's Unit 5. When another page here says “Topic 5.4,” this is where to look it up. The short codes after each key concept (KC-5.2.II.A) are the guide's own numbering, in case you want to find that line in it.

  • Topic 4.9

    The Development of an American Culture

    ARC · American and Regional Culture
    You should be able to
    Explain how and why a new national culture developed from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • A new national culture mixed American, European and regional elements. KC-4.1.II.B
    • Liberal ideas from abroad and the Romantic belief in perfectibility shaped literature, art, philosophy and architecture. KC-4.1.II.C
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 4.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 4–7 (Transcendentalists pod); reading
    Unit 4 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 4.10

    The Second Great Awakening

    ARC · American and Regional Culture
    You should be able to
    Explain the causes of the Second Great Awakening.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Democratic and individualist beliefs, a response to rationalism, and market-revolution change, with more mobility, caused a Protestant revival. KC-4.1.II.A.i
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 5.B.
    When we covered it
    Day 4 (temperance and revival pod); reading
    Unit 4 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 4.11

    An Age of Reform

    ARC · American and Regional Culture
    You should be able to
    Explain how and why various reform movements developed and expanded from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Revival inspired moral and social reform and utopian and religious movements. KC-4.1.II.A.ii
    • Voluntary organizations (temperance and others). KC-4.1.III.A
    • Northern emancipation grew the free Black population while states restricted its rights. KC-4.1.III.B.i
    • Antislavery grew in the North. KC-4.3.II.B.i
    • A women's rights movement, Seneca Falls, and later disputes over narrowing it to white women. KC-4.1.III.C
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Comparison. Skill 3.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 4–7 Movement Media
    Unit 4 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 4.12

    African Americans in the Early Republic

    SOC · Social Structures
    You should be able to
    Explain the continuities and changes in the experience of African Americans from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • In the South, antislavery effort was mostly unsuccessful rebellion. KC-4.1.III.B.ii
    • Enslaved and free Black Americans built communities and strategies, and joined political efforts. KC-4.1.II.D
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Continuity and change. Skill 3.D.
    When we covered it
    Days 6 and 8
    Unit 4 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 4.13

    The Society of the South in the Early Republic

    GEO · Geography and the Environment
    You should be able to
    Explain how geographic and environmental factors shaped the development of the South from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Most Southerners owned no enslaved people, but leaders called slavery the Southern way of life. KC-4.3.II.B.ii
    • Reliance on staple exports built a Southern identity. KC-4.2.III.C
    • Worn-out soil pushed planters and slavery west. KC-4.3.II.A
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 1.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 8 and 11
    Unit 4 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.1

    Contextualizing Period 5

    You should be able to
    Explain the context in which sectional conflict emerged from 1844 to 1877.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Previews KC-5.1 (expansion, migration, debates over rights) and KC-5.2 (slavery divides the nation).
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Continuity and change. Skill 4.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 1 and 15 (pre- and post-thesis)
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.2

    Manifest Destiny

    GEO · Geography and the Environment
    You should be able to
    Explain the causes and effects of westward expansion from 1844 to 1877.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Resources, opportunity and religious refuge drew migrants west. KC-5.1.I.A
    • Manifest Destiny and claims of superior institutions justified annexation, often leading to conflict. KC-5.1.I.B
    • Wartime laws later promoted Western transport and growth. KC-5.1.I.D
    • Trade interest built ties with Asia. KC-5.1.I.E
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 1.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 1, 2 and 10
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.3

    The Mexican–American War

    WOR · America in the World
    You should be able to
    Explain the causes and effects of the Mexican–American War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • War and diplomacy added Western lands, raising questions about slavery, American Indians and Mexicans there. KC-5.1.I.C
    • Federal conflict with Mexican Americans and American Indians grew in the new lands, undermining their self-sufficiency and cultures. KC-5.1.II.C
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 3.C.
    When we covered it
    Day 3
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.4

    The Compromise of 1850

    NAT · American and National Identity
    You should be able to
    Explain the similarities and differences in how regional attitudes affected federal policy in the period after the Mexican–American War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • The Cession set off fights over slavery in the territories. KC-5.2.II.A
    • Courts and leaders tried to resolve it, including the Compromise of 1850. KC-5.2.II.B.i
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Comparison. Skill 4.B.
    When we covered it
    Day 10
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.5

    Sectional Conflict: Regional Differences

    ARC · American and Regional Culture SOC · Social Structures
    You should be able to
    • Explain the effects of immigration from various parts of the world on American culture from 1844 to 1877.
    • Explain how regional differences related to slavery caused tension in the years leading up to the Civil War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Irish and German immigrants settled in ethnic communities. KC-5.1.II.A
    • Anti-Catholic nativism. KC-5.1.II.B
    • Free labor against enslaved labor; the free soil movement. KC-5.2.I.A
    • Abolitionists, a Northern minority, used moral argument, helped escapes and sometimes violence. KC-5.2.I.B
    • Defenders of slavery used racial doctrines, the positive good claim and the Constitution and states' rights. KC-5.2.I.C
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Comparison. Skill 2.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 8, 10 and 11
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.6

    Failure of Compromise

    PCE · Politics and Power
    You should be able to
    Explain the political causes of the Civil War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • The Kansas-Nebraska Act and Dred Scott failed to reduce conflict. KC-5.2.II.B.ii
    • Slavery and nativism weakened party loyalty, ending the Second Party System and creating sectional parties, above all the Republicans. KC-5.2.II.C
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 5.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 9 and 10
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)

  • Topic 5.7

    Election of 1860 and Secession

    PCE · Politics and Power
    You should be able to
    Describe the effects of Lincoln's election.
    Key concepts, in brief
    • Lincoln won on a free soil platform with no Southern electoral votes; after contested debates, most slave states seceded, setting off the war. KC-5.2.II.D
    How the exam asks about it
    Reasoning process: Causation. Skill 4.B.
    When we covered it
    Days 10, 11–14 and 18–19
    Unit 5 in the College Board's course guide (PDF)
