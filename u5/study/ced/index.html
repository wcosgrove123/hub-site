CED topics

The 12 College Board topics this unit covers.

  • Topic 4.9

    The Development of an American Culture

    ARC · American and Regional Culture Causation · Skill 4.B
    Learning objective
    Explain how and why a new national culture developed from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-4.1.II.B: a new national culture mixed American, European and regional elements. KC-4.1.II.C: liberal ideas from abroad and the Romantic belief in perfectibility shaped literature, art, philosophy and architecture.
    Our days
    D4–D7 (Transcendentalists pod); reading
    Unit 4 in the CED

  • Topic 4.10

    The Second Great Awakening

    ARC · American and Regional Culture Causation · Skill 5.B
    Learning objective
    Explain the causes of the Second Great Awakening.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-4.1.II.A.i: democratic and individualist beliefs, a response to rationalism, and market-revolution change, with more mobility, caused a Protestant revival.
    Our days
    D4 (temperance and revival pod); reading
    Unit 4 in the CED

  • Topic 4.11

    An Age of Reform

    ARC · American and Regional Culture Comparison · Skill 3.B
    Learning objective
    Explain how and why various reform movements developed and expanded from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-4.1.II.A.ii: revival inspired moral and social reform and utopian and religious movements. KC-4.1.III.A: voluntary organizations (temperance and others). KC-4.1.III.B.i: Northern emancipation grew the free Black population while states restricted its rights. KC-4.3.II.B.i: antislavery grew in the North. KC-4.1.III.C: a women's rights movement, Seneca Falls, and later disputes over narrowing it to white women.
    Our days
    D4–D7 Movement Media
    Unit 4 in the CED

  • Topic 4.12

    African Americans in the Early Republic

    SOC · Social Structures Continuity and change · Skill 3.D
    Learning objective
    Explain the continuities and changes in the experience of African Americans from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-4.1.III.B.ii: in the South, antislavery effort was mostly unsuccessful rebellion. KC-4.1.II.D: enslaved and free Black Americans built communities and strategies, and joined political efforts.
    Our days
    D6, D8
    Unit 4 in the CED

  • Topic 4.13

    The Society of the South in the Early Republic

    GEO · Geography and the Environment Causation · Skill 1.B
    Learning objective
    Explain how geographic and environmental factors shaped the development of the South from 1800 to 1848.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-4.3.II.B.ii: most Southerners owned no enslaved people, but leaders called slavery the Southern way of life. KC-4.2.III.C: reliance on staple exports built a Southern identity. KC-4.3.II.A: worn-out soil pushed planters and slavery west.
    Our days
    D8, D11
    Unit 4 in the CED

  • Topic 5.1

    Contextualizing Period 5

    Continuity and change · Skill 4.B
    Learning objective
    Explain the context in which sectional conflict emerged from 1844 to 1877.
    Key concepts, in brief
    Previews KC-5.1 (expansion, migration, debates over rights) and KC-5.2 (slavery divides the nation).
    Our days
    D1, D15 (pre- and post-thesis)
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.2

    Manifest Destiny

    GEO · Geography and the Environment Causation · Skill 1.B
    Learning objective
    Explain the causes and effects of westward expansion from 1844 to 1877.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.1.I.A: resources, opportunity and religious refuge drew migrants west. KC-5.1.I.B: Manifest Destiny and claims of superior institutions justified annexation, often leading to conflict. KC-5.1.I.D: wartime laws later promoted Western transport and growth. KC-5.1.I.E: trade interest built ties with Asia.
    Our days
    D1, D2, D10
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.3

    The Mexican–American War

    WOR · America in the World Causation · Skill 3.C
    Learning objective
    Explain the causes and effects of the Mexican–American War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.1.I.C: war and diplomacy added Western lands, raising questions about slavery, American Indians and Mexicans there. KC-5.1.II.C: federal conflict with Mexican Americans and American Indians grew in the new lands, undermining their self-sufficiency and cultures.
    Our days
    D3
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.4

    The Compromise of 1850

    NAT · American and National Identity Comparison · Skill 4.B
    Learning objective
    Explain the similarities and differences in how regional attitudes affected federal policy in the period after the Mexican–American War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.2.II.A: the Cession set off fights over slavery in the territories. KC-5.2.II.B.i: courts and leaders tried to resolve it, including the Compromise of 1850.
    Our days
    D10
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.5

    Sectional Conflict: Regional Differences

    ARC · American and Regional Culture SOC · Social Structures Comparison · Skill 2.B
    Learning objective
    LO E: Explain the effects of immigration from various parts of the world on American culture from 1844 to 1877. LO F: Explain how regional differences related to slavery caused tension in the years leading up to the Civil War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.1.II.A: Irish and German immigrants settled in ethnic communities. KC-5.1.II.B: anti-Catholic nativism. KC-5.2.I.A: free labor against enslaved labor; the free soil movement. KC-5.2.I.B: abolitionists, a Northern minority, used moral argument, helped escapes and sometimes violence. KC-5.2.I.C: defenders of slavery used racial doctrines, the positive good claim and the Constitution and states' rights.
    Our days
    D8, D10, D11
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.6

    Failure of Compromise

    PCE · Politics and Power Causation · Skill 5.B
    Learning objective
    Explain the political causes of the Civil War.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.2.II.B.ii: the Kansas-Nebraska Act and Dred Scott failed to reduce conflict. KC-5.2.II.C: slavery and nativism weakened party loyalty, ending the Second Party System and creating sectional parties, above all the Republicans.
    Our days
    D9, D10
    Unit 5 in the CED

  • Topic 5.7

    Election of 1860 and Secession

    PCE · Politics and Power Causation · Skill 4.B
    Learning objective
    Describe the effects of Lincoln's election.
    Key concepts, in brief
    KC-5.2.II.D: Lincoln won on a free soil platform with no Southern electoral votes; after contested debates, most slave states seceded, setting off the war.
    Our days
    D10, D11–D14, D18–D19
    Unit 5 in the CED
